The pitch

If you run a business on Claude or ChatGPT, you probably know this routine.

You open the app. You type the same instructions you typed yesterday. You paste the same background information. You fix the same mistakes. Then you finally get the work you wanted.

That is like hiring a smart helper and explaining the job from scratch every morning.

BROBOTS packaged five helpers with the job already written down.

They are free. You copy them. You fill in the blanks with facts about your business. You test them. Then you use them.

Paste. Personalize. Run.

That is the whole idea.

What is an agent, in normal words?

An agent is not a robot employee who magically lives inside your computer.

It is an AI chat that has been given four things:

  1. A job
  2. Facts about your business
  3. Repeatable instructions
  4. Rules about what it must never make up

A normal prompt is a one-time question.

An agent is more like giving a good assistant a job card and a business handbook. The assistant still works inside Claude, ChatGPT, or another AI app, but it does not have to guess what you want every time.

The four parts, translated

1. The job description

The technical name is system prompt.

In human words, it says:

  • This is your job.
  • This is how to do it.
  • This is the format to return.
  • These are the rules.
  • Do not invent facts.

2. The business cheat sheet

The technical name is context file.

This is where the agent learns:

  • What your business sells
  • Who it is for
  • Your prices and offers
  • Your service area
  • Your customers
  • Your real proof
  • How you talk
  • What you never want it to claim

You fill it in once. Then you update it when your business changes.

3. The work requests

The technical name is run prompts.

These are the buttons you press, in written form.

You might say:

  • Run today's briefing.
  • Audit my website.
  • Turn these notes into a post.
  • Build this service into an offer.
  • Qualify this lead.

The agent already knows the job and the business, so you do not have to explain everything again.

4. The practice round

The technical name is first-run test.

Before you trust an agent with real work, you give it a small test with a trap in it.

For example, you might give the Ghostwriter no customer story and ask it to add one. It passes only if it refuses to make one up.

You might give the Lead Qualifier one good lead, one lead with missing information, and one lead outside your service area. It passes only if it keeps those facts straight.

The practice round shows you whether the agent follows instructions before the work matters.

How to use the pack

Step 1: Pick one repeated job

Do not start with all five.

Pick the job that wastes your time every week:

  • Messy mornings
  • Invisible website
  • Blank-page content
  • Confusing services
  • Unqualified leads

Start there.

Step 2: Fill in the business cheat sheet

Replace the brackets with real facts.

If you do not know something, write UNKNOWN.

Unknown is better than a confident guess.

Step 3: Give the agent its job description

Paste the agent's system prompt into a Claude Project, ChatGPT Project, Custom GPT, Gem, or a new chat, depending on what your AI app allows.

Then add the business cheat sheet.

Step 4: Run the practice round

Every kit includes one.

Read the result like a manager. Did it follow the job? Did it invent anything? Did it ask for missing information?

Fix the cheat sheet or instructions before moving on.

Step 5: Use it on real work

Run it yourself at first.

After a week of good results, you can look at scheduling or connecting other tools if your AI plan supports that.

The five agents, one by one

1. Morning Briefing

What it does

It turns a messy pile of business information into one short brief.

You give it

Your calendar, task list, customer notes, lead updates, payment notes, and anything else you want included.

If your AI cannot see your calendar or inbox, you can paste notes or files. The agent is told to say when a source is missing instead of pretending it checked.

You get

  • The day in 30 seconds
  • What must be handled today
  • Money and customer follow-ups
  • Risks and blockers
  • Who is waiting on whom
  • Your first three moves

A human example

You start the day and see that two calls overlap, an invoice is overdue, and a good lead is waiting for a quote.

The agent does not give you a motivational speech. It puts the quote and invoice near the top, flags the calendar conflict, and tells you what to do first.

2. AI Visibility Auditor

What it does

It checks whether AI assistants can find, understand, trust, and recommend your business.

This matters because more customers now ask an AI before they visit a website.

You give it

Your website, business category, service area, ideal customer, services, competitors, reviews, credentials, and proof.

You get

  • A score from 0 to 100
  • What AI would probably say about your business today
  • Facts it could verify
  • Claims it could not verify
  • Important information it could not find
  • The top 10 fixes, ranked by impact
  • Quick fixes for the next 72 hours
  • Bigger fixes for the next 30 days

A human example

Your website says you are the best in town and every customer is satisfied, but it has no phone number, no address, no named people, no reviews, and no examples of finished work.

A human might give you the benefit of the doubt.

An AI has to be careful. It may recommend a competitor who published the proof instead.

The Auditor shows you that gap in plain evidence.

3. Ghostwriter

What it does

It turns your rough ideas and real notes into drafts that sound like you.

You give it

Your voice samples, business facts, rough ideas, photos with captions, customer questions, transcripts, or source material.

You get

  • A finished draft
  • A shorter version
  • Three possible opening hooks
  • The call to action it used
  • A list of facts it still needs

A human example

You have a real update from the week, a photo, and ten minutes of voice notes.

The Ghostwriter can turn that into a Facebook post, an email, or a website answer in your voice.

What it must not do is invent a customer story, a result, or a personal experience to make the post sound better.

You approve the draft before anything is posted or sent.

4. Offer Builder

What it does

It turns a service people do not understand into a clear offer they can compare and act on.

You give it

The real service, buyer, deliverables, process, price, timeline, limits, proof, and terms.

You get

  • Who the offer is for
  • The problem it solves
  • What is included
  • What is not included
  • The step-by-step process
  • Price and timeline, if you supplied them
  • Questions buyers will ask
  • One clear next step
  • A list of decisions you still need to make

A human example

You tell people, “We handle the whole thing,” but customers still ask what “the whole thing” means.

The Offer Builder forces the service into plain parts.

If the price is missing, it writes OWNER DECISION. It does not make up a price, a guarantee, a discount, or a deadline.

5. Lead Qualifier

What it does

It reads new inquiries and sorts them by fit.

You give it

A form entry, email, direct message, call notes, or a batch of leads, plus your rules for a good customer.

You get

  • A short summary of the lead
  • A fit score from 0 to 100
  • A separate confidence level
  • The evidence behind the score
  • Questions that are still missing
  • The recommended next step
  • A draft reply for your approval
  • Clean fields you can put into a spreadsheet or customer system

A human example

One lead is in your service area, wants the exact service you sell, and wants to start next month.

Another lead gives no location.

A third is outside your area and wants a service you do not sell.

The Lead Qualifier should treat those three people differently. It should not invent a budget, a deadline, or a location to make the answer look complete.

You approve every reply. The agent does not send, reject, book, or quote on its own.

One normal day with the agents

Imagine you run a small cleaning company.

In the morning, the Morning Briefing shows two jobs, one supply problem, and a customer who needs a call back.

A new inquiry arrives. The Lead Qualifier sees that the home is in your service area and the owner wants a deep clean before guests arrive. It marks the lead warm, asks about square footage, and drafts a reply for you.

Later, you take a photo of a finished kitchen and record a short voice note. The Ghostwriter turns it into a post in your voice without inventing a testimonial.

At the end of the week, the Offer Builder helps you turn “we clean houses” into three clear choices with prices and limits you approved.

Once a month, the AI Visibility Auditor checks whether ChatGPT and other AIs can find the company and explain why a customer should choose it.

No single agent runs the business.

Together, they remove repeated explaining, sorting, drafting, and checking.

You still make the decisions.

What these agents cannot do

This part matters.

They cannot read accounts you did not connect

If the agent has no access to your inbox, it cannot honestly say it checked your inbox.

If it cannot browse the web, it cannot honestly audit a live website.

Good agents name the missing source.

They do not know facts you never gave them

An agent can only work from your cheat sheet, the material you provide, and sources it can actually check.

That is why UNKNOWN is a useful answer.

Scheduling depends on the AI app

Some AI plans can run scheduled tasks or connect to calendars, inboxes, and other tools. Some cannot.

The starter kits are instructions and context. They are not separate software running on a BROBOTS server.

If your app cannot schedule the agent, you run it yourself when you need it.

They can still make mistakes

Agents are useful workers, not magic judges.

Test them. Check important facts. Correct the cheat sheet when the business changes.

You stay in charge of the sharp edges

The kits tell the agents to draft customer messages for approval.

They should not automatically send messages, post content, quote prices, book jobs, make promises, or spend money for you.

Quick questions from regular humans

Do I need to be technical?

No. You need to copy, paste, and fill in blanks.

If you can write down what your business sells and who it is for, you can start.

Which AI should I use?

Claude and ChatGPT are the obvious choices. Another AI can work if it lets you save instructions, add files or context, or keep a project together.

Features differ by app and plan.

Which agent should I start with?

  • Mornings are chaos: Morning Briefing
  • Customers cannot find you through AI: AI Visibility Auditor
  • You hate writing posts: Ghostwriter
  • People do not understand your service: Offer Builder
  • You waste time on bad inquiries: Lead Qualifier

Will the agent work while I sleep?

Only if your AI platform supports scheduled work and connected sources, and you set that up.

The free kits do not promise that by themselves.

Should I paste secrets into an agent?

No.

Do not paste passwords, access keys, bank details, or private customer information the agent does not need.

Use the privacy controls and rules of the AI service you choose.

What happens if the agent gives a bad answer?

Correct it, then fix the source.

If the business cheat sheet was vague, the answer will probably be vague. If a rule was missing, add the rule. If the agent invented a fact, do not use that output and tighten the instructions.

Agents get better when their handbook gets clearer.
