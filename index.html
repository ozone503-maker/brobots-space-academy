The internet is filling up with pages that all have the same gradient, the same three columns, the same sentence about being passionate. They were generated, glanced at, and shipped.

We run the same tools everybody else has. The difference is the step after: a human goes back through and removes everything that reads as machine-made.

That step is the entire job. It's pass 06 below, and it's the one nobody else runs.

How a site gets builtSeven passes · in order · no skipping
01

Direction

Before a pixel exists: what it looks like, what it sounds like, and why. Type, color, imagery, how things move, and the one thing people should remember. Decided up front, on purpose, in writing.

02

The map

Every section gets a job. Nav, opening, the story, the proof, the ask, the form, the footer, and how each one hands you to the next. Phone first, because that's where they'll actually see it.

03

The one image

One picture or idea does the heavy lifting. How it's framed, how it enters, what happens when you scroll past it. If a visitor remembers one thing about your site, this is the thing.

04

Motion

How the page arrives. What moves as you scroll. Hovers, transitions, the small stuff. Every movement has to earn its place. Anything there just to look busy gets cut.

05

Build

Actual construction, in stages. Components, responsive behavior, forms, accessibility, speed, and search, set up to be found on Google and Bing and in AI answers from ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini, and Grok. Each stage tested before the next starts. Nothing that already worked gets broken along the way.

06

The de-slop pass

A human goes through the whole thing hunting for anything generic. Default fonts. Stock-photo energy. Spacing that's technically correct and completely dead. Copy that could belong to any company on earth. It all gets ripped out and replaced with something that could only be yours.

07

Launch audit

Real devices, real browsers, real load times. Forms that actually send. Links that actually go. Everything ranked by what it costs you if it stays broken, then fixed. Then it ships.

What does Brobots Space Factory do?

Brobots Space Factory is a web studio in Mountain View, Hawaiʻi. We build websites, do SEO, and work on AI visibility so businesses get found on Google and named by AI assistants like ChatGPT, Grok, and Perplexity. We also build custom AI chatbots called BroBots.

How much does a custom website cost?

A custom website starts at $495. Website tiers run from $495 to $4,995, with à la carte add-ons. A custom BroBot chatbot is $495.

What is AI visibility?

AI visibility is how often AI assistants name and link your business when people ask them for a recommendation. We measure it with the Space Factory Visibility Audit, and work to improve it alongside classic SEO. No one can promise a ranking or a mention.

Is Brobots Space Factory the same as the Brobots books, podcast, or robotics teams?

No. Brobots Space Factory at brobots.space is a Hawaiʻi web, SEO, and AI visibility studio founded by Jessie Sponberg. It is not connected to the Brobots children's graphic novels, the BroBots podcast, or any FIRST or VEX robotics team.
