05

What the AIs found, and what they could not find

What is there

  • Real services: negotiating electricity rates, fixing contract language, correcting power factor penalties, and tax-exempt studies.
  • Big claimed results: 95% on rate and contract improvement, 99% on power factor correction, 93% on contract language.

What is missing

  • A phone number. There is none anywhere on the site.
  • An email address you can read. The only contact route is a form.
  • People. No names, no photos, no team. Chris, the person the video tells buyers to email, appears nowhere.
  • Proof. The 95, 99, and 93 numbers are stated, but there is no story, no example, and no customer behind any of them.
  • The $10,000 story. The best proof the company has lives in a video that is not on the site.
  • Room to breathe. Every service is squeezed onto one single page, so no service gets properly explained.
  • Fresh dates. The site still says 2019, and its Texas energy history stops around 2016. To an AI, that reads like a company that may no longer exist.
  • The state registration. Texas energy brokers are registered with the Public Utility Commission of Texas (PUCT). Showing that registration is basic trust, and buyers and AIs both look for it.
  • The Google listing. The map and business listing that Google shows for a company name search has not been claimed and built out.

None of this says the company is weak. It says the company is unverifiable, and unverifiable is the one thing an AI will not recommend.
